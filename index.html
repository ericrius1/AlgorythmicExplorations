About this site

Understanding complex systems by running them

Algorythmic Explorations is a library of interactive essays built around a single habit: if you want to understand a phenomenon, build the simplest working version of it, watch where it breaks, and fix one bottleneck or add one piece of physics at a time. The galaxy spinning behind the title above is 20,000 bodies pulling on each other right now on your machine—not a video, not an illustration. Every figure across the site works the same way: drag it, poke it, push a slider until the math falls apart, and see why the next algorithm was invented.

Most explanations stop at static diagrams or hide the machinery behind libraries. Here, the simulations run live in your browser from first principles—mostly in hand-written WebGPU compute shaders and real-time Web Audio, without external physics or ML engines. Each series starts from a single rule and scales it up step by step: two falling dots become a cosmic web, a lone sine wave grows into a band that listens and plays along, a pure noise function erodes into an endless mountain range, and tiled matrix multiplications grow into a language model trained right inside your tab.

Although the eleven series span astrophysics, fulldome optics, light transport, magnetic matter, music, room acoustics, procedural terrain, solar physics, neuroscience, and machine learning, the same core ideas keep reappearing across them. Spatial grids that sort water also sort lava-lamp wax and ferrofluid; Fourier transforms that solve cosmic gravity also dissect musical timbre; GPU compute pipelines that manage a million firework sparks also multiply attention matrices. You can read straight through from Part 1 of Particle Worlds, or jump into any series below—the first essay of each opens with an overview of the territory and what you'll build together.

Built from scratch Every solver, shader, and synth starts from the underlying equation—no pre-rendered clips or hidden engines.
Made to be poked Drag canvases, sweep parameters, and watch live readouts. Figures pause automatically when scrolled out of view.
Sound & WebGPU The music and acoustics essays generate live audio once you tap; the heavy GPU figures shine in a WebGPU browser.
