Architecture · Stage 1Pretraining vs. Fine-Tuning: how a blank mind gets a voice

If you ever wondered how companies turn a raw next-token predictor into a helpful assistant with a consistent personality — instead of a chaotic autocomplete engine — the six companions above use the exact same two-stage recipe at miniature scale:

  1. Stage 1 · Pretraining (general language): Starting from random Gaussian weights, one shared base Transformer — the winning 780K-parameter architecture discovered by Part 7's AutoResearch run (6 layers, dModel = 128, 4 heads, SwiGLU, context = 128) — reads millions of characters of clean cozy English stories (TinyStories) plus general conversation on an NVIDIA L4 GPU. During pretraining, the weights learn spelling, punctuation, sentence rhythm, and everyday English structure, dropping validation bits-per-byte down to ~1.29 bpb (saved as companion-base.smw).
  2. Stage 2 · Fine-Tuning (bespoke personality): Starting from that shared companion-base.smw mind — never from scratch! — six separate copies are trained for a brief, gentle sprint on species-specific user: … / pet: … dialogue tailored to each creature's voice: sleepy sunbeam purrs for Mochi, moonlit riddles for Kitsune, mossy pond splashes for Pebble, starry astronomy notes for Pip, zoomies and embers for Bramble, and translucent harmonic ripples for Luma. On-character validation drops to ~0.17 bpb while preserving general English fluency (~1.29 bpb)!

Each finished species brain is then group-quantized to Part 7's compact Q8 .smw format (~857 KB each — roughly the size of a single medium JPEG photo!) and shipped statically right alongside the page. When you pick a species above, your browser fetches its .smw file, unpacks all 780,000 weights straight onto WebGPU storage buffers, and starts generating replies live on your graphics card. (If WebGPU is unavailable or a custom checkpoint isn't found, PetBrain gracefully falls back to a nimble 48D scratch model that warm-starts on the spot.)

Architecture · Stage 2Continual learning without catastrophic forgetting

A static pretrained pet quickly feels scripted, yet updating a live neural network while someone chats with it immediately runs into one of deep learning's hardest unsolved puzzles: catastrophic forgetting (the stability–plasticity dilemma).

Imagine your companion already speaks fluent cozy English, and you type "My name is Maya and I love telescopes!" If you immediately run 50 aggressive gradient steps exclusively on that one sentence, backpropagation happily drives the loss on "Maya and telescopes" down to zero — by hijacking and overwriting the weights that used to know how to say hello, answer questions, or form ordinary grammar! After two chats, every reply turns into garbled repetitions of your last message.

PetBrain balances stability and plasticity with two practical defenses:

  • An episodic replay buffer: Rather than training on your newest line in isolation, every chat exchange gets appended to a personal memory bank. Online gradient steps sample mini-batches from a blend of the species' core personality seed, general pet dialogue, and your personal memories (gently repeated so your name and topics carry weight without drowning out English).
  • Conservative micro-updates: Whenever you chat with a pretrained 780K brain, the companion first runs real autoregressive WebGPU decoding (temperature = 0.65, top-p = 0.9), captures its live Layer-0 attention heatmap, and then executes just 3 tiny gradient steps at lr = 0.0008 (roughly 10× gentler than scratch training!) before persisting the updated Q8 checkpoint to IndexedDB.

Open the Brain Peek tab above after a few messages to inspect the real WebGPU readouts: backend, checkpoint source, architecture, parameter count, live Layer-0 causal attention map, raw WebGPU token sample, and a Train +15 Steps button to watch online loss shift live.

Architecture · Stage 3Teaching moves with reinforcement learning (policy gradients)

Every training loop in Parts 1 through 7 used supervised learning (or self-supervised next-token prediction): for every input, the dataset already knew the exact right answer letter so cross-entropy could grade the error directly.

Teaching physical tricks works differently! When you teach a puppy to spin or bow, you do not hand the puppy a spreadsheet of exact muscle torques — you let it try a move and hand out a treat when it does what you hoped! That paradigm — learning from sparse scalar rewards rather than labeled answer keys — is Reinforcement Learning (RL).

Inside the Teach Moves tab, six procedural moves (Hop, Spin, Sing, Zoomies, Bow, and Starflip) sit behind a compact policy weight table mapping companion state to action logits. When you cue a move and click Reward ✦, a REINFORCE-style policy gradient step raises that trick's policy weights and mastery score in proportion to how much room remains to learn: 

// Policy gradient intuition (REINFORCE):
// If action 'a' earned a positive reward R, push log-probability log π(a) UP!
Δθ  ∝  +R · ∇θ log π_θ(action)

As mastery climbs, your companion's autonomous sampling distribution shifts — whenever they feel happy and idle, they begin performing their favorite rewarded moves spontaneously on their own, earning XP and unlocking higher visual evolution stages!

Aside · How trick rewards connect to RLHF in frontier AI

Modern AI assistants go through a remarkably similar third stage right after pretraining and fine-tuning: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (and verifiable reasoning RL). Instead of only predicting the average next word on the internet, the model generates complete candidate answers, receives a scalar reward score (from human raters, a reward model, or unit tests passing!), and uses policy gradients to make high-reward reasoning paths more probable and unhelpful ones less probable. Rewarding Mochi for a starflip uses the exact same core idea!

Architecture · Stage 4Recursive self-improvement while dreaming

Neuroscientists have long noticed that sleeping mammalian brains do not simply switch off — during quiet rest, the hippocampus rapidly replays snippets of the day's experiences so the cortex can consolidate new memories without breaking old ones.

Tap Start Dream Cycle ☽ inside the Dream (AutoResearch) tab above. The sanctuary lighting dims to twilight, your companion curls down, closes its eyes, and breathes slowly while Part 7's Browser AutoResearcher wakes up in the background! It runs a real UCB-bandit WebGPU trial over your companion's personal replay buffer, logs the architectural hypothesis and validation val_bpb, consolidates the live brain with a calm replay burst, and saves the newly consolidated weights to IndexedDB before your creature wakes up wiser (and a little closer to its next evolution!).

Architecture · Stage 5Five worlds and six evolving rigs

Every sanctuary backdrop borrows an algorithmic idea from another series across Algorythmic Explorations:

  • Meadow Valley (Ground Truth) — procedural terrain contour, hundreds of instanced wind-swaying grass blades, and drifting twilight fireflies.
  • Starlight Nebula (Particle Worlds) — a floating crystal plinth ringed by Keplerian orbital dust motes that accelerate when your companion plays.
  • Bonfire Clearing (Bounced Light) — a flickering warm campfire sending luminous embers curling into the dark sky.
  • Harmonic Hall (Living Music & Acoustic Spaces) — concentric acoustic floor rings that pulse with gentle pentatonic Web Audio chimes whenever your companion hops or sings.
  • Ferro Sanctuary (Strange Matter) — a glossy obsidian magnetic pool that ripples and raises Rosensweig-style spikes whenever your companion moves or you tap the floor.

Each of the six starter species is a self-contained procedural 3D WebGPU rig built with SDF-sculpted geometry, backlit subsurface scattering, instanced fur shells, filmic AgX tone mapping, and HDR bloom. Every rig implements a shared SpeciesDefinition contract — smooth look-at eye tracking following your pointer or tossed orb, natural blinks, happy crescent squints when petted, a neural aura tied directly to live WebGPU attention energy, and four visual evolution stages (Hatchling → Curious Sprout → Radiant Friend → Starlight Sage) unlocking collars, floating constellations, spirit fox tails, and celestial crowns.

Three of the six starter species (Mochi, Kitsune, and Pebble) were modeled by Claude Opus 5.5 and three (Pip, Bramble, and Luma) by Gemini 4 Neon — press ; at any time to inspect the active creature's model author alongside frame rate and live WebGPU metrics.
